# Source check: contested rules and Rioplatense TTS (2026-10-23)

Status: research done, no rule changed yet. Each finding says what was read.

## 1. /s/ before a pause, and /s/ in general

**Terrell, T. D. (1978). "La aspiración y elisión de /s/ en el español
porteño". *Anuario de Letras* 16, 41–66.** Read in full
(<https://revistas-filologicas.unam.mx/anuario-letras/index.php/al/article/download/388/386>).
Data: 24 educated porteños (Norma Culta project), 12 women and 12 men, ages
25–75, semi-informal conversation, 14,703 tokens of /s/.

| Context (Cuadro II) | [s] | [h] | ∅ | N |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Word-internal before C | 12% | 80% | 8%* | 4150 |
| Word-final before C | 11% | 69% | 20% | 5475 |
| Word-final before V | 88% | 7% | 5% | 2649 |
| Word-final before pause | 78% | 11% | 11% | 2407 |

\* The OCR of Cuadro II reads 3%; the text gives 8% as the average.

- Conclusion (p. 61): "Si le sigue una vocal o una pausa, la norma es la
  sibilante; de otra manera se da la aspirada."
- Before /t/ the sibilant survives more often, often as a mixed [hs].
- Deletion is highest before a consonant (20%), in polysyllables, and in
  redundant plural /s/; *entonces* loses its /s/ in 42% of cases.
- Fontanella de Weinberg (1973), Bahía Blanca, as summarized by Terrell:
  upper-middle speakers avoided pre-pausal deletion entirely when reading.
  In conversation the rate was 2% for women and 25% for men; lower classes
  deleted more.

**Coloma & Colantoni, "La aspiración de /s/ en el español rioplatense: tango
vs. rock"** (<https://ucema.edu.ar/~gcoloma/aspiracion-tango-rock-alfal.pdf>).
Read in full. Preconsonantal /s/ in 34 recordings (701 tokens) was aspirated
83% of the time in tango and 4% in rock. Andrés Calamaro aspirated 73% in
tango and 6% in rock, so the style effect is large. The authors excluded
consonant clusters (*instante*, *extraño*) as too rare.

**Consequence for the transcriber:**
- The default [s] before a pause is confirmed.
- The casual option "/s/ → [h] before a pause" describes a minority
  variant (22% weakened in Terrell, split evenly between [h] and deletion).
  Wikipedia's "strongly tends toward aspiration before a pause in popular
  speech" is not supported for educated speech. Lower-class speech was not
  read directly.
- Not modelled yet: deletion of word-final /s/ before a consonant (20%).

## 2. /s/ in a two-consonant coda (*abstracto*, *instante*, *perspectiva*)

No Rioplatense data found. Coloma & Colantoni excluded these clusters, and
Coloma's IPA Illustration keeps [ks] in *extraño* `neks·'tɾa·ɲo`. A general
syllabification manual (<http://elies.rediris.es/elies4/Fon2.htm>, via
search snippet) says /bs/, /ns/, /rs/ in learned prefixes (*substancia*,
*instinto*, *perspicaz*) may reduce to /s/. That is not Buenos Aires data.
**Status: unverified.** The current output (*abstracto* → [aβhˈtɾakto])
stays an inference.

## 3. Word-final and intervocalic /d/

No Buenos Aires study found.
- Díaz-Campos & Gradoville (2011, HLS 13,
  <http://www.lingref.com/cpp/hls/13/paper2490.pdf>): read the data
  sections. Its /d/ deletion data is from **Caracas** (7,200 tokens, 10.1%
  deleted, highest in *-ado*). Its Buenos Aires data covers /ʒ/ devoicing
  only.
- A secondary overview citing Lipski (1994: 190) says Montevideo and Buenos
  Aires resist intervocalic /d/ elision more than Santiago or Asunción,
  except in the lowest strata. Not read in the original.

**Status: unverified for final /d/.** The "drop final -d" option has no
Rioplatense source.

## 4. Infinitive-final /ɾ/

Gilbert & Rohena-Madrazo (2017), "Revising the canon: Social and stylistic
variation of coda (-ɾ) in Buenos Aires Spanish", *Romance Languages and
Linguistic Theory* 12, ch. 5. The full text was not accessible (HTTP 403).
From search snippets of the abstract: the approximant is the most frequent
realization of coda /ɾ/, deletion occurs, and lenited forms are commoner in
informal speech and among younger, male and working-class speakers. Whether
infinitives were analysed separately is unknown.

**Status: secondary.** It is the right source, but it still needs reading.
